# 2. Product Vision

The application is a personalized, adaptive guitar learning platform that functions as an intelligent personal guitar teacher.

Most guitar learning applications focus on predetermined courses, song libraries, tablature, video lessons or performance scoring.

This application should combine those useful capabilities with a comprehensive understanding of each individual student.

The application should continuously understand:

- Who the student is musically.
- What they already know.
- What they believe they know.
- What they can actually demonstrate.
- What they want to accomplish.
- What is preventing them from progressing.
- What they should learn next.
- How they should practice it.
- Whether they have retained and internalized the knowledge.
- How to adjust their training based on actual performance.

The application should help users become musically independent rather than dependent on memorized exercises, tablature or instructional videos.

It should accommodate complete beginners, self-taught guitarists, intermediate players and advanced musicians, while initially focusing on developing an exceptional experience for an advanced-beginner rock guitarist.

The initial user will be the founder himself.

Example skill: Perfect Fifth Intervals.

Practical application: Power chords, rock riffs and chord construction.

Exercise: Locate and play perfect fifths from several different root notes.

Assessment: Independently identify, construct and play perfect fifths from unfamiliar roots.

Transfer assessment: Apply perfect fifths when creating an original riff or transposing a power chord progression.

Related skills: Power chords, triads, intervals and harmonic relationships.

# 11. Mastery and Proficiency System

Progress must be based on demonstrated competence rather than lesson completion alone.

A proposed five-stage mastery framework:

## Level 1: Introduced

The student has encountered the concept.

## Level 2: Recognized

The student can identify or perform it with assistance.

## Level 3: Demonstrated

The student can independently demonstrate the skill under familiar conditions.

## Level 4: Applied

The student can use the skill in a practical musical context.

## Level 5: Internalized

The student can apply the skill reliably across different musical situations without excessive conscious effort.

Example: Minor pentatonic scale.

Introduced: The student has seen the scale shape.

Recognized: The student can reproduce the familiar pattern.

Demonstrated: The student can play the scale independently and identify its notes.

Applied: The student can improvise over an appropriate chord progression.

Internalized: The student can transpose the scale, navigate different positions, target chord tones and use it musically in unfamiliar situations.

The five-level framework should be researched and refined.

Different skills may require different assessment dimensions.

For example, theoretical knowledge, physical technique, auditory recognition and creative application should not necessarily be measured identically.

Track proficiency confidence, supporting evidence, last assessment date and retention.

Avoid arbitrary progress percentages that are not grounded in meaningful measurements.

- What the student is learning.
- Why it matters.
- How it relates to their goals.
- What they should physically do.
- How long they should practice.

- Five minutes of warm-up.
- Fifteen minutes of applied theory and fretboard knowledge.
- Ten minutes of rhythm or repertoire.

Every practice session should combine appropriate amounts of:

- Knowledge acquisition.
- Physical execution.
- Musical application.
- Retrieval.
- Assessment.
- Enjoyment.

Avoid making practice feel like a collection of disconnected exercises.

A student should understand how today's session contributes to becoming the musician they want to become.

## Important Technical Limitations

A detected pitch does not uniquely identify the string or fret used.

Multiple guitars, distortion, bends, slides, effects and alternate tunings complicate transcription.

Generated tablature should support confidence indicators, user correction and alternative fingerings.

Do not promise perfect transcription.

Investigate copyright, music licensing and platform terms before implementing link-based imports.

Initially support user-owned recordings, original music and authorized audio uploads.

Do not assume the application can legally download or transcribe arbitrary commercial songs from streaming platforms.

# 27. Educational Quality Standards

Every educational experience should satisfy the following principles:

1. Teach something meaningful.
2. Explain why it matters.
3. Connect it to the student's ambitions.
4. Demonstrate the concept clearly.
5. Provide practical application.
6. Require appropriate student participation.
7. Assess understanding.
8. Identify misconceptions.
9. Encourage retention.
10. Support transfer into actual musical situations.

Avoid:

- Excessive theoretical explanations without playing.
- Unnecessary beginner material.
- Memorization without understanding.
- Generic AI-generated exercises.
- Arbitrary skill percentages.
- Premature advancement.
- Excessive repetition without purpose.
- Overwhelming students with too many concepts.
- Rigid curricula that ignore demonstrated ability.
- Treating lesson completion as mastery.
- Overpromising audio assessment capabilities.

The application should balance structured improvement with enjoyment and musical creativity.
